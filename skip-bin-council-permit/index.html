Skip bin permits in Geraldton: City of Greater Geraldton rules

A skip on your own driveway usually doesn't need council approval. A skip on the road, footpath or nature strip is different. Here's what City of Greater Geraldton says and what to check before you book.

Last checked 24 October 2026

The short answer

On your own land, generally no. On a road or verge, yes: the City of Greater Geraldton requires a Licence to Obstruct a Thoroughfare or Verge, which covers skip bins and other bulk containers.

On your own property

Your own driveway, yard or a firm patch of ground is the easiest place for a skip. The City's licence is for skips on a road or verge, so a bin wholly on your property generally doesn't need it. On sandy blocks, ask the operator about boards under the bin and make sure the truck won't sink.

On the road, footpath or nature strip

The City of Greater Geraldton requires a Licence to Obstruct a Thoroughfare or Verge before anything is placed on a road or verge, and the licence covers bulk storage containers such as skip bins. Conditions can include protective barriers and lighting at night, and the City may ask for a deposit or charge for any damage to the verge. The City doesn't publish the fee online, so ask when you apply.

Before you book a skip on the street or verge, check with the City of Greater Geraldton on (08) 9956 6600 or council@cgg.wa.gov.au, or visit the City's website. Mention it on the quote form too, because the operator may handle the licence or advise on placement.

What to ask council before you book

  1. Does my spot need a Licence to Obstruct a Thoroughfare or Verge, and who should apply, me or the skip operator?
  2. What does it cost this financial year, and how long does assessment take?
  3. How long can the skip stay, and are there conditions such as lights, reflectors or a set distance from the kerb?
  4. Are there extra rules for a busy road, a bus stop or a heritage area?

Ask for the answers in writing or note the date and who you spoke to.

Placing the skip safely

Keep the skip clear of driveways, footpaths, hydrants and drains, and don't fill above the rim. If it's on the verge, use the barriers and night lighting the licence asks for. Given the licence conditions and possible deposit, it's usually worth finding room on your own property.
